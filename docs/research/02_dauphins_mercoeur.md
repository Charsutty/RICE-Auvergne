# Dauphins of Auvergne and Mercœur

## Working thesis

The relationship between the Dauphins of Auvergne and the lords of Mercœur offers a strong dynastic narrative because the two houses are connected through marriage, inheritance, and court culture rather than merely being neighboring rivals.

## Documented anchors

### Division of the comital house — A

Pierre-François Fournier describes the twelfth-century division of the county of Auvergne between two members of the comital house, both named Guillaume. The descendants of Guillaume the Elder retained the comital title, while the descendants of Guillaume the Younger gradually adopted the title of **Dauphin d'Auvergne**.

Source: https://www.persee.fr/doc/bec_0373-6237_1930_num_91_1_448896

Gameplay consequence: the emergence of the Dauphins should be represented as a dynastic and territorial rupture, not as an ahistorical title permanently present from game start.

### Dauphin as a dynastic marker — A

The IRHT authority record for Dauphin d'Auvergne (1150–1234) identifies him as son of Guillaume VII of Auvergne and Marquise d'Albon, daughter of Guigues IV d'Albon. The name/surname “Dauphin” is tied to this maternal connection and later evolves into a title associated with the Auvergnat line.

Source: https://personnes.irht.cnrs.fr/84701

Gameplay consequence: an event chain can connect maternal prestige, naming, branch identity, and the eventual adoption of a distinctive title.

### Marriage bridge between Mercœur and the Dauphins — A

The IRHT/Sigilla record for **Alays de Ventadour** states that she first married Guillaume I of Mercœur and, after his death, married Robert I Dauphin in 1238. She had children from both unions.

Source: https://sigilla.irht.cnrs.fr/49121

Gameplay consequence: this is an excellent model for events showing how aristocratic networks overlap and how a widow can connect two major houses.

### Mercœur inheritance by the Dauphins — needs stronger primary-source verification

Older reference works state that the original Mercœur line became extinct in the fourteenth century and that the seigneurie passed by inheritance to the Dauphins of Auvergne, counts of Clermont.

Preliminary reference: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Merc%C5%93ur%2C_Seigneurs_and_Dukes_of

Evidence class for implementation: **B until verified through a stronger medieval/modern scholarly source**.

Gameplay consequence: potential later-game inheritance chain, but not yet ready for scripted historical enforcement.

## Court culture connection

A scholarly review of the troubadour Peirol notes that Dauphin d'Auvergne was apparently his principal patron and that one poem was sent to the countess of Mercœur, described as Dauphin's sister in the literary tradition discussed by the scholar.

Source: https://www.persee.fr/doc/roma_0035-8029_1956_num_77_308_3060_t1_0522_0000_1

This suggests a useful narrative network linking the Dauphin court, Mercœur, Polignac, and troubadour culture, while requiring caution where the literary biographies are uncertain.

## Design directions

### Major chain: The Divided House

Possible stages:

1. Succession dispute in the comital house.
2. Territorial compromise or war.
3. Formation of a cadet political identity.
4. Adoption of the “Dauphin” dynastic marker/title if historical conditions develop.
5. Patronage and marriage politics distinguish the branch.

The chain must support counterfactual outcomes: reconciliation, complete victory by one branch, or a different partition.

### Mercœur–Dauphin relationship

Possible content:

- marriage negotiations between highland lords and the Clermont/Dauphin network;
- widowhood and remarriage creating cross-house claims;
- inheritance of strategically important lordships;
- courtly patronage linking Mercœur, Clermont, and Polignac;
- rivalry over influence rather than simplistic permanent hostility.

## Avoid

- Hard-coding Mercœur and the Dauphins as hereditary enemies.
- Creating the Dauphiné d'Auvergne in 867 or 1066 as if its later identity already existed unchanged.
- Treating later inheritance claims as certain until stronger sources are found.

## Open research questions

- Exact territorial partition between the elder and younger comital branches.
- Chronology of the title “Dauphin d'Auvergne” in diplomatic usage.
- Exact genealogical route through which Mercœur passed to the Dauphin line.
- CK3 representation of these families at 1066 and later dates.
