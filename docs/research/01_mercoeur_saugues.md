# Mercœur, Saugues, and the northern Gévaudan frontier

## Working thesis

Saugues should be represented neither as the political center of the flavor pack nor as an irrelevant peripheral easter egg. Its historical value lies in its position inside the seigneurial and fortified network of Mercœur, at the junction of the Velay, Gévaudan, Margeride, and Auvergnat highlands.

## Documented anchors

### Mercœur and the canton of Saugues — A

A nineteenth-century scholarly review of Augustin Chassaing's *Spicilegium Brivatense* notes that the documentary collection covers not only the Brivadois and Auvergne but also the portion of Gévaudan depending on the **sirie of Mercœur**, corresponding to the modern canton of **Saugues**.

Source: https://www.persee.fr/doc/bec_0373-6237_1886_num_47_1_447459_t1_0308_0000_4

Gameplay consequence: Mercœur content can legitimately target the Saugues area without treating Saugues as an independent major principality.

### Fortified landscape — A

Archaeological survey around Saugues covers a highland zone between the Devès and northern Margeride. Work using the early-fourteenth-century *Terrier des Mercœur* identified numerous deserted and fortified medieval sites. The survey interprets castles as structuring settlement and describes a network of fortifications along the Allier and tributaries controlling access to this frontier zone under the direct authority of Mercœur or its vassals.

Source: Christian Sermet, archaeological survey report, https://www.persee.fr/doc/arcme_0153-9337_2000_num_30_1_1637_t1_0384_0000_2

Gameplay consequence: suitable basis for events about castellans, tolls, route security, disputed fortification rights, isolated communities, and winter logistics.

### Mercœur as an Auvergnat ecclesiastical network — A

Odilon of Mercœur, abbot of Cluny from 994 to 1049, is a major figure linking the Mercœur family to wider Auvergnat and European monastic networks. A scholarly colloquium volume explicitly frames Odilon through “Auvergne and Cluny” and the Peace of God around the year 1000.

Source review: https://www.persee.fr/doc/rhef_0048-7988_2003_num_89_222_4046_t16_0189_0000_1

Gameplay consequence: the family should not be represented solely as militarized highland castellans; ecclesiastical patronage and church careers are central to its flavor.

## Design directions

### Strong candidates

- **Mercœur frontier network**: a set of events/modifiers rather than a large bespoke UI.
- **Control the Upper Allier crossings**: local route-security and toll content.
- **Castles of the high country**: events where local lords or vassals fortify strategic sites.
- **A son for the Church**: dynasty members can gain prestige and networks through ecclesiastical careers.
- **Peace in the highlands**: Peace of God / monastic mediation content around the eleventh century.
- **Saugues regional flavor**: rare local events, not a global decision visible to every ruler.

## Avoid

- Treating Saugues as equivalent in weight to Clermont or Le Puy.
- Treating the modern Margeride identity as a fixed medieval political unit.
- Giving Mercœur a permanent “mountain warrior” bonus unsupported by the sources.
- Assuming every fortified site belonged directly to the main Mercœur line.

## Open research questions

- Exact chronology and territorial extent of Mercœur lordship from the tenth to thirteenth centuries.
- Which CK3 baronies best map to the sirerie at each bookmark.
- Historical status of Saugues itself within the Mercœur network at 867 and 1066.
- Relationship of Mercœur to Brioude and Le Puy in ecclesiastical and political terms.
- Reliable reconstruction of the Mercœur genealogy before implementing characters.
