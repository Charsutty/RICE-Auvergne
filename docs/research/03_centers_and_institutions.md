# Major centers and institutions

## Purpose

This note anchors the regional weighting of the mod. Mercœur/Saugues content should exist inside a wider Auvergnat system whose major political and religious centers often had greater historical reach.

## Clermont — political and episcopal center

### Étienne II of Clermont — A

Jean-Pierre Chambon and Christian Lauranson-Rosaz describe Bishop Étienne II of Clermont (mid/late tenth century) as one of the most important pre-Gregorian bishops of the diocese. He came from the noble lineage of the viscounts of Clermont, acted in royal and regional politics, and played an important role in the Peace of God context.

Source: https://www.persee.fr/doc/anami_0003-4398_2002_num_114_239_2775

Gameplay relevance:

- bishop/count tension should be structural, not cosmetic;
- elite families can place members in episcopal office;
- ecclesiastical authority can act as public/order-making authority;
- Peace of God content is especially appropriate for the late tenth/eleventh centuries.

## Brioude — comital and canonical network

### Carolingian political importance — A

The cartulary of Saint-Julien de Brioude preserves an unusually rich body of ninth- and tenth-century material. Ferdinand Lot uses its charters to reconstruct the counts of Auvergne and notes that Count Bernard was also lay rector of Brioude in the mid-ninth century.

Source: https://www.persee.fr/doc/bec_0373-6237_1941_num_102_1_449257

Léonce Auzias likewise describes the Brioude cartulary as a major source for Carolingian Aquitaine, preserving more than 250 ninth- and tenth-century charters.

Source: https://www.persee.fr/doc/anami_0003-4398_1932_num_44_176_5156

Gameplay relevance:

- Brioude is a strong 867 anchor;
- lay aristocratic involvement in Saint-Julien can connect political office and religious patronage;
- the chapter can support character/dynasty content rather than functioning merely as a building bonus.

### Noble chapter — A

Modern scholarship explicitly emphasizes the aristocratic character of the chapter of Saint-Julien.

Source review: https://www.persee.fr/doc/bec_0373-6237_1982_num_140_1_450260_t1_0113_0000_3

Gameplay relevance:

- noble canons;
- prestigious ecclesiastical placements;
- disputes over patronage and chapter rights;
- dynastic memory and burial.

## La Chaise-Dieu — reform and expansion

### Foundation and early expansion — A

La Chaise-Dieu was founded by Robert of Turlande in 1043; Robert died in 1067 and was canonized shortly afterward. Scholarship places the abbey's rapid expansion within the eleventh-century reform movement and notes its spread outward from Auvergne.

Sources:
- https://www.persee.fr/doc/cafan_0575-061x_1984_act_19_1_1353
- https://www.persee.fr/doc/anami_0003-4398_1914_num_26_104_8010

Gameplay relevance:

- excellent 1066-era content because the institution is still young;
- patronage can accelerate a growing religious network;
- dependencies/priories can be represented through events and modifiers rather than map ownership.

## Le Puy / Velay — connected but not Auvergne proper

The Velay has distinct political history and should not be collapsed into Auvergne. Carolingian scholarship treats counts of Auvergne and counts of Velay as related but distinct problems.

Source: https://www.persee.fr/doc/anami_0003-4398_1904_num_16_63_3617

Gameplay relevance:

- Le Puy should be a connected regional pole, especially for routes, church networks, and eastern highland politics;
- Saugues can mediate between Velay and Mercœur/Gévaudan content;
- avoid simply relabeling Velay as “eastern Auvergne.”

## Design hierarchy resulting from the evidence

1. **Clermont** — major political/episcopal system.
2. **Brioude** — major aristocratic/religious and 867 system.
3. **Aurillac / La Chaise-Dieu** — major monastic flavor depending on chronology.
4. **Le Puy / Velay** — external but deeply connected pole.
5. **Mercœur / Saugues / northern Gévaudan** — meaningful frontier and dynastic network with focused local flavor.

This hierarchy should guide content density, not prohibit local events.
