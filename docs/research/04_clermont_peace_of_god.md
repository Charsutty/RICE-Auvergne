# Clermont, episcopal authority, and the Peace of God

## Purpose

This note supports the `Counts and Crosiers` module and defines the evidence boundary for late tenth- and eleventh-century peace-making flavor around Clermont.

## Étienne II of Clermont — A

Jean-Pierre Chambon and Christian Lauranson-Rosaz describe Étienne II, bishop of Clermont in the tenth century, as a major pre-Gregorian bishop drawn from the local aristocracy. He participated in royal and regional politics and belongs to the political-religious environment from which the Peace of God developed in Auvergne.

Source:
- https://www.persee.fr/doc/anami_0003-4398_2002_num_114_239_2775

Gameplay implications:

- episcopal authority can be represented as part of public order and aristocratic politics rather than only spiritual counsel;
- elite kinship and ecclesiastical office should eventually intersect in character content;
- Clermont flavor should concern jurisdiction, mediation, rights, and legitimacy rather than a simple clergy-opinion bonus.

## Peace of God in Auvergne — A for the regional phenomenon, C for specific event reconstruction

The same scholarship places Étienne II and Auvergne within the late tenth-century environment of the Peace of God. The movement sought to restrain forms of aristocratic violence and protect churches and vulnerable groups through ecclesiastical authority, assemblies, and sworn peace.

For this project, that supports a regional political-religious event in the late tenth and eleventh centuries.

It does **not** by itself support inventing a specific Clermont council, exact oath text, named participants, or a permanent institutional mechanic.

Implementation boundary:

- `auvergne.1002` is Class C gameplay reconstruction;
- its chronology is limited to 975–1099 as a deliberately conservative gameplay window;
- localization must state that it represents the broader environment rather than one documented Clermont assembly.

## Open questions
