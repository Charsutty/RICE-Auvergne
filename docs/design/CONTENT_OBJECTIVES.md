# Content objectives

## Purpose

RICE: Auvergne should make playing in medieval Auvergne feel meaningfully different from playing a generic French or Occitan ruler, while remaining proportionate to the region's historical importance and faithful to RICE's focused flavor-pack philosophy.

The project should not become a catalogue of local references. Each feature should answer a design question:

> What makes political, dynastic, religious, and social life in medieval Auvergne distinct enough to create different CK3 stories?

The pack therefore prioritizes systems and narrative networks over raw geographic coverage.

---

## Objective 1 — Represent fragmented authority

The central political theme is that holding the main title does not mean exercising uncontested authority over the whole region.

Relevant actors include:

- the comital house;
- bishops, especially Clermont;
- major aristocratic lineages;
- castellans and local lords;
- monastic and canonical institutions;
- urban communities;
- outside princes and kings.

### Gameplay target

Create recurring choices between centralization, compromise, patronage, coercion, and recognition of local privileges.

Typical content:

- jurisdictional disputes;
- confirmation of privileges;
- fortification rights;
- episcopal opposition;
- urban bargaining;
- arbitration between local powers;
- consequences for legitimacy, control, opinion, prestige, and piety.

### Design question

> How far can the ruler impose authority without uniting local powers against them?

This is the main political spine of the flavor pack.

---

## Objective 2 — Give the comital house and Dauphins a dynamic dynastic history

The emergence of the Dauphins of Auvergne should be represented as a consequence of dynastic division, inheritance, branch identity, and territorial rivalry rather than as a static title that simply exists from game start.

### Gameplay target

Build a major dynamic event chain around:

- succession disputes;
- cadet branches;
- partition;
- rival claims;
- marriage diplomacy;
- maternal prestige and naming;
- branch identity;
- eventual adoption of the Dauphin identity/title where appropriate.

### Counterfactual outcomes

The player should not be forced into the historical outcome. Possible results should include:

- the historical split;
- reconciliation;
- victory of one branch;
- an alternative partition;
- extinction or political marginalization of one branch.

This should be the pack's strongest explicitly dynastic narrative.

---

## Objective 3 — Make religious institutions political actors

Religious centers should matter because they participate in aristocratic networks, education, patronage, mediation, property, and legitimacy.

Priority institutions:

1. Clermont;
2. Brioude;
3. Aurillac;
4. La Chaise-Dieu.

Le Puy belongs to the wider regional network and should influence Auvergne without becoming the main subject of the pack.

### Gameplay target

Develop patronage as a recurring relationship rather than a one-time piety purchase.

Possible actions and events:

- finance a religious institution;
- donate property or revenue;
- seek prayers and commemoration;
- place or educate a relative in the Church;
- recruit learned clergy;
- use an abbot or bishop as mediator;
- defend ecclesiastical privileges;
- challenge those privileges;
- compete with another dynasty for patronage.

The player should gain political and dynastic opportunities as well as piety.

---

## Objective 4 — Represent the highlands as a social and political system

The Upper Allier, Mercœur, Saugues, the Velay, the Margeride, and the northern Gévaudan should not be central merely because they are locally interesting. Their value is that they reveal how lordship operates outside the main urban and ecclesiastical centers.

Mercœur is the primary bridge between local highland flavor and the larger Auvergnat dynastic world.

### Gameplay target

Use the vanilla map and existing title scopes to represent:

- route control;
- river crossings;
- tolls;
- castles and castellans;
- local vassal networks;
- winter logistics;
- isolated communities;
- pilgrimage traffic;
- pastoral movement;
- frontier arbitration.

### Saugues rule

Saugues should receive **limited but precise** flavor.

Good implementation:

- a few well-researched events tied to Mercœur, the Upper Allier, routes, fortifications, or local lordship.

Bad implementation:

- a bespoke county;
- disproportionate global decisions;
- treating Saugues as equal in political weight to Clermont or Le Puy.

---

## Objective 5 — Let Auvergnat identity emerge from gameplay

The project should not begin by defining Auvergne primarily as a new culture with a package of bonuses.

Instead, identity should emerge from:

- fragmented lordship;
- local privileges;
- dynastic networks;
- highland geography;
- religious patronage;
- Occitan court culture;
- repeated interaction with neighboring powers.

### Possible later content

A decision such as **Affirm the Customs of Auvergne** may eventually represent the political or cultural consolidation of this identity.

If a cultural divergence mechanic is used, it should be the result of accumulated regional conditions, not the foundational feature of the mod.

---

## Objective 6 — Keep Auvergne connected to the wider world

Auvergne should never feel like an isolated micro-setting.

Relevant external networks include:

- Aquitaine;
- the Capetian monarchy;
- Bourbon and the Bourbonnais;
- Limousin;
- Languedoc;
- Velay;
- Gévaudan;
- Cluny;
- pilgrimage routes;
- the wider Occitan aristocratic world.

### Gameplay target

Create events where outside relationships materially affect local politics:

- royal intervention;
- requests for homage or support;
- marriage opportunities;
- Cluniac or monastic mediation;
- troubadour patronage;
- inheritance across regional boundaries;
- pilgrimage traffic;
- neighboring dynasties seeking influence.

The region should function as a crossroads, not an enclave.

---

# Content modules

The six objectives should be implemented through five coherent modules.

## Module A — Counts and Crosiers

Focus:

- Clermont;
- comital authority;
- episcopal power;
- jurisdiction;
- privileges;
- Montferrand in the appropriate chronology.

Role in the pack:

**Primary political spine.**

---

## Module B — The Divided House

Focus:

- the comital dynasty;
- succession conflict;
- emergence of the Dauphin branch;
- partition and reconciliation;
- marriage and inheritance.

Role in the pack:

**Primary dynastic narrative.**

---

## Module C — Saints and Patrons

Focus:

- Clermont;
- Brioude;
- Aurillac;
- La Chaise-Dieu;
- Le Puy as an external/regional node;
- ecclesiastical patronage and education.

Role in the pack:

**Primary religious and intellectual system.**

---

## Module D — Lords of the High Country

Focus:

- Mercœur;
- Upper Allier;
- Velay;
- Saugues;
- northern Gévaudan/Margeride;
- castles, routes, tolls, and highland lordship.

Role in the pack:

**Secondary regional module with strong local flavor and links to the main dynastic systems.**

---

## Module E — Courts of Auvergne

Focus:

- troubadours;
- Occitan aristocratic culture;
- reputation of courts;
- marriage networks;
- literary patronage;
- satire, prestige, and rivalry.

Role in the pack:

**Court and cultural flavor connecting Auvergne to the wider Occitan world.**

---

# Priority tiers

## Tier 1 — Essential

These features define the pack and should exist before a 1.0 release.

- comital authority versus Clermont's ecclesiastical power;
- comital dynasty and Dauphin branch;
- religious patronage as a recurring system;
- Mercœur as a meaningful highland lineage/network;
- generic highland events that can apply across relevant vanilla counties.

## Tier 2 — Strong flavor

These features deepen the main systems.

- Montferrand;
- Brioude;
- Aurillac;
- La Chaise-Dieu;
- troubadours and court patronage;
- Le Puy and pilgrimage connections;
- Bourbon and Gévaudan relationships.

## Tier 3 — Enrichment

These features should be added only after the pack's main systems are coherent.

- additional historical characters;
- minor aristocratic lineages;
- highly localized events;
- Saugues-specific flavor;
- Polignac;
- Apcher and Randon where relevant to cross-border stories;
- detailed pastoral and local economic flavor.

---

# Scope discipline

A proposed feature should normally be rejected or redesigned if it:

- requires a map change;
- is interesting only because of modern regional identity;
- gives a minor locality disproportionate importance;
- duplicates generic French or Occitan flavor without an Auvergnat angle;
- creates permanent bonuses where events and relationships would tell the story better;
- has no connection to characters, dynasties, institutions, travel, titles, or other CK3 systems;
- would make little sense to a player who does not know the region beforehand.

---

# Success criteria

A successful campaign in or around Auvergne should naturally generate stories involving several of the following:

- conflict or compromise with an ecclesiastical power;
- a succession dispute inside the comital house;
- emergence or suppression of a cadet branch;
- patronage of a major religious institution;
- a local fortress or route becoming politically important;
- marriage connecting Auvergne to another regional power;
- a Mercœur-related highland event;
- pilgrimage or monastic networks affecting local politics;
- Occitan court culture and literary patronage;
- intervention by a stronger outside ruler.

No single story should be mandatory.

The target is not historical reenactment. The target is **historically grounded emergent gameplay**.

---

# Development order

Recommended implementation sequence:

1. **Counts and Crosiers** — establish the political baseline.
2. **The Divided House** — build the core dynastic chain.
3. **Saints and Patrons** — add the religious patronage loop.
4. **Lords of the High Country** — connect Mercœur/Saugues and the Upper Allier to those systems.
5. **Courts of Auvergne** — add broader Occitan court flavor.
6. Add Tier 3 local content only after the main modules are working together.

This order keeps the pack useful to any Auvergne-focused player while preserving precise local flavor where it is historically justified.
